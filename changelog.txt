pair_recon Driver-side window statistics, Right log capture, and row-level differences — 2026-10-29

Summary
- Right window statistics of a window with at most `RIGHT_STATS_DRIVER_ROWS` (2000000) sampled rows are projected by Spark as normalized text and reduced in pandas on the driver; larger windows keep the batched Spark aggregates.
- Each Right window is materialized once as a persisted temp view (partition + sample predicate) and released after its statistics, sample, and rows are taken, so a source relation that takes minutes to analyze is planned and scanned once per window instead of once per query.
- Spark queries become plain projections sized by `RIGHT_STATS_DRIVER_CELLS` (5000000 cells per scan), so a few-thousand-row window of 867 columns is fetched in one scan instead of 18; at most `RIGHT_DRIVER_FETCHES` (1) window holds a pandas frame on the driver at once, keeping the peak near 750 MB on a 15 GB driver.
- Each distinct text is hashed once (sha256 first 16 hex digits weighted by its count), matching the Left DuckDB and Spark aggregate results exactly.
- After comparison the Right streams every sampled row of each differing window for its differing columns into `rows.csv.gz` (normalized key, raw and normalized values) partition by partition, never holding the result on the driver; the Left joins them with its local window CSV in DuckDB on the normalized key and writes the first `sample_rows` row-level differences (different, left only, right only) as `differences/<vintage>/rows.xlsx` on the Volume and beside `left.csv` locally.
- The Right notebook buffers its log in memory and rewrites `_protocol/right.log` on the Volume every 30 seconds from a daemon thread (20 MB cap, failed writes retried, never raised into the job); the Left copies it to `right.log` beside `left.log` once the job ends.
- Numeric columns canonicalize equivalent decimal text before hashing and row comparison, so representations such as `0.19`, `.19`, `01.20`, `1.2`, `1e3`, and `1000` compare consistently across Oracle, Hadoop, CSV, and Databricks without rounding distinct high-precision values together.
